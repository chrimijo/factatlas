# Module — Products, models, technologies and components

## Exact identification

Manufacturer, brand, family, reference/variant code, production date/territory, generation, market, non-personal serial identifier if needed, parent/successor links.

## Common research questions

1. Origin, design, team, purpose and launch/commercialization context.
2. Dated manufacturer specifications, units, standards and revisions; distinguish advertised claims from independent tests.
3. Architecture, components, materials, dimensions, consumption/performance, interfaces, compatibility, operating environment and limitations.
4. Families, references, revisions, generations, market variants, editions and dates: technical differences matrix.
5. Innovations: establish what is new, patented or inherited, with exact attribution.
6. Reliability: recalls/safety notices, documented defects, symptoms, causes, triggering conditions, manufacturer fixes, part revisions and uncertainty of prevalence.
7. Operation/maintenance: servicing, parts, repairability, software support/obsolescence, regulatory requirements, sourced indicative costs dated and localized.
8. Commercial details: original list price, verified sale price, secondary market and alternatives; asking prices are not completed transactions.
9. Documentation: manuals, service bulletins, approvals, data sheets, technical announcements and independent laboratory tests.

## Pitfalls

References, markets and model years matter; commercial synonyms do not prove identical hardware. Frequent forum reports do not establish systematic failures or statistical rates. A country's recall is not automatically worldwide. Laboratory claims do not establish real-world performance.
