# Module — Geographic places

## Establish boundaries first

Municipality, city, village, canton, region, district, valley, natural site, urban area or metropolitan area; coordinates and dated boundaries. Resolve homonyms, exonyms and boundary changes.

## Research

- Physical geography: coordinates, relief, hydrology, climatological normal and period, environment and natural hazards.
- Administrative status, subdivisions, borders, authorities and dated territorial reforms.
- Documented etymology, local history, settlement chronology and heritage.
- Area, population, density and demographic change: exact boundary definition and statistical year required.
- Economic activities, socioeconomic demographics, transport and services where sourced.
- Culture, languages, institutions and notable sites; do not reduce the report to tourism.
- Environmental issues, protection, resources and recent events.

## Sources and risks

National/cantonal mapping services, public cadastres, statistical registers, local authorities, archives, geographic institutes, weather offices, heritage authorities and scientific publications. Separate municipality/urban area, central point/territorial boundary, summit/average elevation, resident/tourist population and historical/current borders. Never invent geolocations.
