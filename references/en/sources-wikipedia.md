# Source policy: Wikipedia as an index leading to evidence

## Source hierarchy

1. Verifiable primary evidence: public acts/registers, audited accounts, regulatory filings, contemporary archives, standards and approvals, manufacturer documentation, workshop manuals, patents, original scientific research, heritage authorities, statistical institutes and court documents.
2. Qualified secondary evidence: academic studies, historians, specialist journalists, museums, documented technical analysis and publications with explicit methods.
3. Research indexes: Wikipedia, Wikidata, collaborative databases, forums and aggregators. These offer leads; they are insufficient evidence for decisive claims.

## Wikipedia workflow, whenever feasible

1. Find a relevant accessible article. If none is available, disclose that limitation and continue through other sources.
2. Read it for chronology, names, controversies, references and external links; record version/date where relevant.
3. Examine bibliography, footnotes, primary documents and cited secondary sources, including relevant accessible language editions.
4. Actually open and read the relevant original documents. A Wikipedia reference that was not opened cannot be cited as a source personally consulted.
5. Evaluate author/institution, purpose, date, proximity, independence, conflicts of interest, scope, method, access and corrections.
6. Cross-check significant, disputed or changing claims against other evidence. Different language editions and translations are not independent corroborations.
7. Retain only wording supported by the document; correct or discard Wikipedia claims that the cited original does not support.
8. Cite the document actually used beside the claim. Cite Wikipedia exceptionally to describe its own editorial presentation, not as ultimate proof of summarized facts.

Wikidata helps identify names, identifiers, dates, relationships and register links; it does not validate those properties automatically.

## Evidence register

Record claim ID, fact, scope, validity date, unit, source URL/identifier, primary/secondary/index type, access date, locatable passage, corroboration, status (established, attributed, disputed, not established) and caution. Record source language, original title, clearly labeled translated title if used, and whether the original was actually checked. Use `../../assets/en/registre-preuves.csv`.

## Independence and scope

Syndicated copies of a press release count as one origin. Check official indicators' definitions. Advertising and catalogues establish advertised specifications; independent tests establish measured performance under their test conditions. A national recall is not automatically global: specify market, manufacture dates and versions. Financial figures need group/company scope, currency, fiscal year, standards and audited/estimated status. Never invent deep links or claim unread pages, images or PDFs were consulted.

## Language and access

Follow [language policy](langues.md): search French, English and relevant original-document languages while writing the entire report in the chosen language. Preserve authentic names, titles and quotes; label translations. If only a translated relay was accessible, attribute it and disclose missing original verification. When browsing is unavailable state “External research not performed”; any provisional report must say what sources are available. Do not assign high confidence to unverified current figures.
